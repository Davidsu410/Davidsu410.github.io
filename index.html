About

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Sociology at the University of California, Berkeley.

Current Work

My current research investigates collective action among gig workers in China, treating gig worker strikes as deviant cases that challenge prevailing theoretical expectations. I examine the conditions under which platform algorithms — designed to discipline and surveil labor — inadvertently facilitate strike mobilization, and how the gamification of work can give rise to a new kind of bargaining power that lets gig workers exert leverage against the platform.

This project has so far produced two papers:

1) “Gaming the Algorithm: Tactical Innovation and Collective Resistance in China’s Platform Economy” (David Su, 2025, under review), which won the 2026 Thompson Graduate Student Paper Award, the 2026 Meyer Zald Distinguished Contribution to Scholarship Award, and the 2026 Herbert Blumer Prize.

2) “The Platforms of Contention: Algorithmic Organization of Work and Gig Worker Strikes in Post-Pandemic China” (David Su, 2025, in progress), supported by the Dr. C.F. Koo and Cecilia Koo Chair Fellowship.
